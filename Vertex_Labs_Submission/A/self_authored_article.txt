Singapore-headquartered cybersecurity startup Meridian Shield has raised US$18 million in a Series B round co-led by Cedar Gate Capital and Harbour Lantern Ventures. The company announced the completed financing on Tuesday and said the new capital will support product development, customer support and recruitment. River Arc Partners participated in the round. It was not described as a lead investor.

Meridian Shield sells software that helps businesses manage access to cloud applications. Its headquarters remain in Singapore, although it has customers in Australia and plans to establish a sales team in Japan. Chief executive Lena Rao said the geographic expansion would be financed from the newly announced capital. The article did not describe the Japanese sales office as a new headquarters or a separate company.

The latest round follows a US$6 million Series A completed in 2024, when Stone Orchard Ventures was the lead investor. Stone Orchard did not participate in the Series B. Meridian Shield has now raised US$27 million across all its funding rounds. A person familiar with the transaction put its valuation at US$120 million. The valuation and lifetime funding figure are provided as background; neither is the amount raised in Tuesday's announcement.

The company said that demand for access controls had grown as smaller firms adopted more cloud services. It plans to use some of the money to improve onboarding and to offer support in additional languages. Rao did not provide a breakdown of the spending. She also said Meridian Shield was considering a small acquisition, but no transaction had been signed and no acquisition price was announced.

In a separate announcement, India-headquartered agritech startup Field Lantern closed a ₹24 crore seed round. The release named Rain Path Ventures and Open Meadow Angels as participants and did not identify any lead investor. It provided no USD equivalent. Field Lantern develops farm management tools and will use the proceeds for field trials. Its founders said local distributors would help recruit farms to participate, but those distributors were not described as investors.

Field Lantern's announcement contained no information about its valuation or earlier financing. A reporter asked whether the round had been led by Rain Path Ventures. A spokesperson declined to assign a lead role and repeated that both named investors had participated. The company said it would publish further operational details after the first trials, without promising an additional financing announcement.

Those discussions remain preliminary: it has not closed or announced a new round. Another company, Route Archive, has agreed to be acquired for US$35 million. That transaction is an acquisition of existing shares and the report does not announce new funding for Route Archive.
